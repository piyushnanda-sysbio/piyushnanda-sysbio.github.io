Why do genetically identical cells, sitting in the same flask, commit to different metabolic states — and what keeps those states stable? I use theory, molecular genetics, and quantitative microscopy to dissect the biochemical circuits that set metabolic state and growth when the environment will not hold still.

Currently I am a PhD candidate in the Biological and Biomedical Sciences program at Harvard University, advised by Andrew Murray. My thesis asks how a bistable switch in mitochondrial activity lets a clonal yeast population hedge its bets across fluctuating environments.

I am an incoming ELISIR Scholar at the EPFL School of Life Sciences, where I will start my own group. The program takes scientists directly from the doctorate to running an independent lab, and mine will ask how cells sense and control glycolytic flux to hold ATP steady when the environment changes faster than gene expression can respond.

Before graduate school I worked with Johan Paulsson at Harvard Medical School on gene expression noise in fast-growing bacteria, with Nozomu Yachie at the University of Tokyo on high-throughput protein interaction mapping, and with Michaela TerAvest at Michigan State University on engineering bacterial central metabolism. I hold a B.Tech and M.Tech in Biotechnology and Biochemical Engineering from IIT Kharagpur.
