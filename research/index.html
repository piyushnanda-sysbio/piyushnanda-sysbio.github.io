Metabolic bistability in yeast

PhD work · Murray Lab, Harvard · 2020–present

A clonal population of budding yeast is not metabolically uniform. Individual cells settle into distinct, heritable states of energy metabolism and stay there for many generations. I combined single-cell metabolic imaging with physiological perturbations to show that this split is generated by a bistable switch in mitochondrial activity, driven by antagonism between cytoplasmic and mitochondrial translation.

Because the two states differ in how they fare when conditions change, the switch gives the population a bet-hedging strategy: it pays a cost in average growth rate in exchange for having some cells already in the right state when the environment turns over. Alongside the experiments, I developed theory and computational pipelines for tracking single cells and their metabolic state over long timescales.

From single cells to multicellular clusters

Murray Lab, Harvard · 2020–2024

Snowflake-like yeast clusters form when daughter cells fail to separate from their mothers. What sets the size and shape of the resulting clusters? I built a dynamic network model that takes cell-level properties — budding geometry, bond strength, division timing — and predicts the full distribution of cluster sizes and compositions, then tested it against high-throughput microscopy measurements of absolute cluster size.

In related work with Jordi Barrere, we showed that alternating selection for dispersal and for multicellularity favors the evolution of regulated life cycles, rather than a fixed compromise between the two.

Genome-scale metabolic modeling

Metabolic Engineering Lab, IIT Kharagpur · 2018–2020

Metabolic networks are large and underdetermined, which makes it hard to say which reactions actually change between two conditions. I designed Genome Scale–Differential Flux Analysis (GS-DFA), a statistical framework that integrates genome-scale metabolic networks with transcriptomic data to identify reactions whose flux is significantly altered, and applied it to lung cells infected with SARS-CoV-2.

Earlier, I reconstructed and analyzed a genome-scale metabolic model of Lachancea kluyveri, a weak Crabtree-positive yeast, to understand how its fermentation–respiration tradeoff differs from that of S. cerevisiae.
